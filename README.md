## Welcome

Starting research involves more than reading papers or learning to use a particular method. It requires asking meaningful questions, evaluating evidence, learning from uncertainty, and explaining your findings clearly.

This handbook provides an accessible starting point for undergraduate students, postgraduate researchers, and anyone beginning their research journey.

The guidance is especially relevant to artificial intelligence, natural language processing, multimodal learning, and healthcare applications. Many of its principles also apply across other research fields.

## How to Use This Handbook

### If You Are New to Research

Begin with **Understanding Research**, then work through the chapters in order.

Complete the checklists using a small research topic or a paper recommended by your supervisor.

### If You Already Have a Project

Choose the chapter that addresses your current challenge.

For example:

- Struggling to understand the field? Start with **Finding Research Papers**.
- Reading without generating questions? Try **Reading Papers Critically**.
- Unsure what to investigate? Work through **Developing Research Ideas**.
- Preparing a lab presentation? Use **Presenting Your Research**.
- Experiments becoming difficult to track? Review **Effective Research Habits**.

### If You Mentor Other Students

Use individual chapters as discussion material.

Ask students to bring reading notes, a short idea brief, or a proposed experiment. Adapt the guidance to your field and supervision arrangements.

## What Each Chapter Includes

The chapters combine:

- Clear explanations of the main concepts.
- Practical steps.
- Illustrative examples.
- Common mistakes.
- Templates or reflection prompts.
- A checklist for applying the guidance.

Examples are teaching aids. They should not be interpreted as established research findings unless explicitly supported by a cited study.
