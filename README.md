## Why this guide exists

Starting research can feel overwhelming. Papers, tools, and advice are everywhere, but understanding how they fit together takes practice. What should you read first? How do you identify a worthwhile question? How do you turn experimental results into a convincing paper?

This English edition makes the original Research Starter Kit easier to navigate for an international audience. It organizes its research and writing topics into a learning path, with particular relevance to students working in artificial intelligence, natural language processing, multimodal learning, and healthcare applications.

## Who this is for

- Undergraduate students beginning their first research project.
- Master's and PhD students joining a research group.
- Researchers developing their academic reading and writing skills in English.
- Mentors looking for a starting resource to share with new students.

## How to use this guide

1. **Build a foundation.** Learn to search the literature, read critically, and keep useful notes.
2. **Develop a question.** Identify a specific limitation or uncertainty and decide what evidence would address it.
3. **Work systematically.** Discuss progress regularly and record experiments and decisions.
4. **Communicate the evidence.** Build the paper around a clear question, justified methods, and supported conclusions.
5. **Revise thoughtfully.** Use feedback to strengthen the research and explain your responses clearly.

These reading suggestions are editorial additions, rather than translations of the linked chapter contents.

## About the editor

I am **Hussain Ahmad**, a PhD scholar in **Control Science and Engineering** at **Beijing University of Chemical Technology**. My research interests include AI for healthcare, medical imaging, multimodal learning, natural language processing, and large language models.

My previous work includes Urdu multimodal named entity recognition. Through this English edition, I aim to make practical research guidance more accessible to students who read and write in English, while preserving clear credit to the people who developed the original resource.
